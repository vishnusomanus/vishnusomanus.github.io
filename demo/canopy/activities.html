Trails, paddling & programs

Lace up at your site and you’re on a trail in two minutes. The kayak rack is at the bottom of the beach stairs, and something’s on at the fire ring most days of the season.

Hiking

Five trails from camp

Showing 5 trails of 5.

  • Bunchberry Bog Boardwalk

    Blue blazes · starts behind site E3

    Distance
    0.8 mi
    Climb
    2 ft
    Level
    Easy

    A level boardwalk loop through a tamarack bog, with benches, two viewing decks and signs about the pitcher plants and sundews. Wheelchair and stroller friendly; the best place on the property to see a moose track.

    Boardwalk, level and wheelchair-friendly

  • Thimbleberry Creek Loop

    Green blazes · starts by Balsam Cabin

    Distance
    1.6 mi
    Climb
    34 ft
    Level
    Easy

    Up one side of the creek and back down the other, over two footbridges and past the little falls. In late July the trail is lined with the thimbleberries it’s named for; pick a handful, leave plenty for the bears.

    Packed dirt and two footbridges

  • Loon Point Trail

    Orange blazes · starts at the beach stairs

    Distance
    2.7 mi
    Climb
    94 ft
    Level
    Moderate

    West along the bluff, down to a sand cove and out onto the rocky point where a pair of loons nests most summers. Roots and a few scrambles; give the nesting area the space the signs ask for.

    Roots, sand and a rocky point

  • Old Tote Road

    Brown blazes · starts at the office

    Distance
    5.2 mi
    Climb
    170 ft
    Level
    Moderate

    The road lumber crews used to haul supplies into camp in the 1890s, now a grassy lane through second-growth maple and hemlock. Rolling and wide enough to walk side by side; good for trail running and fall color.

    Old logging road, grassy and rolling

  • Juneberry Ridge Climb

    Purple blazes · starts on Old Tote Road

    Distance
    3.9 mi
    Climb
    394 ft
    Level
    Hard

    Steep granite to a bald ridge 400 feet above the lake, with two short hand-over-hand pitches near the top. The view runs from the Huron Mountains to Granite Island. Allow three hours and bring water.

    Steep granite, with two hand-over-hand pitches

Paddling

When the big lake lies down, go

Lake Superior can be glass at dawn and whitecaps by lunch. Teemu posts the morning’s paddling call on the board outside the store by 7 am: green, yellow or stay on shore. The creek mouth is sheltered on all but the windiest days.

Single kayak
$35 a day
Tandem kayak
$55 a day
Add a kayak to your stay

  • Sheltered · 1 mi round trip

    Thimbleberry Creek mouth

    Paddle up the creek as far as the first riffle, watching for otters and kingfishers. The calmest water we have, and the right first trip for kids.

  • Open shore · 3 mi round trip

    Loon Point and the sea caves

    West along the beach to the point and the little sandstone caves beyond it. Only on a green-flag morning, staying within 200 yards of shore.

  • Guided only · 5 mi round trip

    Agate Bay crossing

    A morning crossing of the bay with Teemu for confident paddlers, with a beach stop for agates and lunch. Book at the store the day before.

Safety first: the water stays in the 50s°F well into August. Life jackets are worn, not stowed; children under 14 paddle with an adult; and if the flag by the stairs is red, the boats stay on the rack.
