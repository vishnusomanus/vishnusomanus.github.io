Dental implants

What to expect, step by step

  1. 1

    Consultation & 3D scan 45 minutes

    We check your gums and bone, take a low-dose 3D scan and talk through your options, costs and timeline. No commitment needed.

  2. 2

    Implant placement 1–2 hours

    Under local anesthetic (or sedation if you prefer), Dr. Alvarez places a small titanium post where the root used to be. Most people are back at work the next day.

  3. 3

    Healing 3–6 months

    The implant bonds with your jawbone. You’ll wear a temporary tooth so nobody can tell, and we check in at 2 and 8 weeks.

  4. 4

    Your new crown 2 short visits

    We scan the healed site and fit a custom ceramic crown, color-matched to your teeth. It looks, feels and works like a natural tooth.

Aftercare

First 48 hours

  • Rest and keep your head slightly raised
  • Soft, cool foods: yogurt, soup, smoothies (no straws)
  • Use a cold pack on your cheek, 15 minutes on and off

First two weeks

  • Rinse gently with warm salt water after meals
  • Brush carefully around the area with a soft brush
  • Avoid smoking, which slows healing

This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow the personal aftercare instructions your dentist gives you, and call us if you have bleeding, swelling or pain that gets worse.

Questions about implants

Does getting an implant hurt?

The area is fully numbed, so most people feel pressure rather than pain. Afterwards, mild soreness for a few days is common and usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief. Your dentist will advise what is right for you.

How long do implants last?

With good brushing, flossing and regular check-ups, implants can last for decades. The crown on top may eventually need replacing after 10–15 years of wear.

Am I a good candidate?

Most healthy adults are. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and gum disease can affect healing, so we’ll look at your health history at the consultation and suggest alternatives if an implant isn’t the best fit.

Will insurance pay for it?

Many plans cover part of the cost, often the crown. Grace will check your benefits before treatment and give you a written estimate.
