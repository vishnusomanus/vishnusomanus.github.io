The Bearing Method

A compass, not a script.

Good coaching feels like a conversation, but it shouldn’t be aimless. The Bearing Method is the structure underneath: four points we always cover, in roughly this order, so you leave with changed habits rather than good intentions.

The framework

Four points of the compass

Select a point to see what happens there. Most engagements loop round the compass two or three times.

North · usually weeks 1–3

Notice: where are you, really?

Before we change anything we get an honest picture. Not just how you think things are going, but how your calendar, your team and your peers experience you.

  • A one-week calendar audit: decide, inform or habit
  • Short 360° interviews with people you choose
  • Your leadership story: what got you here, and what won’t get you further

East · usually weeks 3–5

Envision: where are you heading?

“Be more strategic” isn’t a goal. We describe the leader you want to be in a year in behaviors your team would actually notice, then pick the two that matter most.

  • A one-page leadership brief you can share with your manager
  • Two focus behaviors, each with a visible signal of progress
  • A clear “stop doing” list

South · the middle of the engagement

Strengthen: build the missing skills.

This is the practical bit. We rehearse the conversations, redesign the meetings and write the delegation plans, using your real situations rather than case studies.

  • Role-play for the conversations you’re dreading
  • Tools: the delegation ladder, the feedback note, the decision log
  • Short reading and listening picked for your situation

West · every week, between sessions

Walk it: practice in the real job.

Change happens on a Tuesday afternoon, not in a coaching session. Each week you run one small experiment and tell me how it went, so new habits get tested while the stakes are still low.

  • One experiment a week, written in a single sentence
  • A two-minute Friday check-in by message
  • A closing review and a plan for the next six months

Inside a session

Sixty minutes, well spent.

Every session has the same shape, so your energy goes into the conversation rather than wondering what comes next.

0 min15304560

  1. 10 minutes

    Arrive

    What’s on your mind today, and what would make this hour useful?

  2. 15 minutes

    Review

    How last week’s experiment went, what surprised you, what you’d change.

  3. 25 minutes

    Work it

    One live problem, properly: questions, a framework if it helps, rehearsal if it’s a conversation.

  4. 10 minutes

    Commit

    Design next week’s experiment and write it down in one sentence, in your words.

A typical engagement

Six months, start to finish.

This is the Executive program. Foundations follows the same path in three months, with fewer loops round the compass.

See 1:1 programs and prices

  1. Week 0

    Free intro call

    Twenty minutes to see whether we’re a fit. If we are, you get a short proposal and a start date the same day.

  2. Weeks 1–3 · Notice

    Assessment and 360° interviews

    A 90-minute kickoff, the calendar audit and up to eight confidential interviews with people you work with.

  3. Weeks 4–5 · Envision

    Your leadership brief

    We turn what we heard into two focus behaviors and an optional three-way conversation with your manager.

  4. Months 2–5 · Strengthen and walk it

    Every-other-week sessions and weekly experiments

    The core of the work, including one shadowing session where I sit in on a real meeting and debrief with you after.

  5. Month 6

    Closing review

    A short repeat of the 360°, a look back at the brief and a written plan for the six months after we finish.

  6. 3 months later

    Check-in, on the house

    One more session to see what stuck, what slipped and what you want to do about it.

Your part

What you’ll need to bring

  • Ninety minutes every two weeks

    The session plus a little time for the weekly experiment.

  • Honesty

    Including about the experiments you didn’t get round to.

  • One real problem

    Coaching works best on the thing you’re actually stuck on.
