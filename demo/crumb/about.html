Our story

It started at a kitchen window

In the spring of 2017, Ruth Ellery was baking four loaves a night in a row house on Beekman Street and leaving them on the windowsill with an honesty box. By the summer it was forty loaves and a line down the sidewalk.

Two years later she took over an old shoe repair shop on Phila Street, installed a deck oven that barely fit through the door, and Bramble & Rye was born. The name came from the brambles in the back yard and the rye that went into her first starter.

“Bread should be worth getting up for.” — Ruth

The sourdough

36 hours, start to finish

Our country loaf is just flour, water, salt and Mabel, the rye starter Ruth has fed every day since 2016. Time does the rest: the long, cold rise is what gives the crumb its custardy shine and the crust its blisters.

  1. Day 1, 9am

    Feed Mabel

    The starter gets rye flour and warm water and wakes up bubbly by lunchtime.

  2. 2pm

    Mix & rest

    Greenfield Mill flour and water sit together for an hour before the salt goes in.

  3. 3–6pm

    Stretch & fold

    Four sets of folds by hand, every 45 minutes, to build strength.

  4. 8pm

    Shape & chill

    Shaped into linen baskets and left overnight in the cold room at 39°F.

  5. Day 2, 4am

    Into the oven

    Scored, steamed and baked dark. Out at 7:15am, on your table by breakfast.

Suppliers

Most of it comes from down the road

We buy from people we can visit, and we pay them on time.

  • Greenfield Mill

    Flour · 14 miles

    Stoneground heritage wheat, spelt and rye from a water mill on Kayaderosseras Creek.

  • Fish Creek Dairy

    Butter & milk · 9 miles

    Cultured butter for our croissants, churned twice a week.

  • Wren Hollow Farm

    Free-range eggs · 11 miles

    Six hundred hens, one very opinionated farm dog.

  • Kettle Lane Orchard

    Apples & plums · 6 miles

    Northern Spies, Stanley plums and a press for the apple juice in the café.

  • High Rock Coffee

    Coffee · 1 mile

    Roasted in an old freight shed by the tracks and delivered by bike.

  • Loughberry Lake hives

    Honey · 2 miles

    From a beekeeping club that meets at the brewpub on Thursdays.

Nothing wasted

Yesterday’s bread has a second life

Preorders mean we bake closer to what you’ll eat. What’s left becomes almond croissants, French toast and breadcrumbs, and anything still on the shelf at closing goes to the Spa City Community Pantry.
