Craft

Varnish, twice a year

Leela Nair · 9 min

The shop is one room with a bench, a window, and a pot that should not be left. Twice a year the oil is cooked. The smell reaches the street and the neighbors know not to ask for a rush job that week. The varnish is the week.

Instruments come in with cracks that have been lived with and cracks that have not. The second kind gets the conversation. The first kind gets a note in a book and is handed back if the player wants it that way. Not every mark is a fault. Some of them are the reason the instrument was bought.

We were allowed to watch a bridge being fitted and not allowed to watch the oil. That part happens early, with the door shut, because the mixture is the part of the work that is still a recipe rather than a method. The bridge, by contrast, is a conversation the maker is happy to have in front of a stranger.

The violin in the photograph belongs to a student who will collect it on Friday. The strings were left on so the photograph would show the instrument as it plays, not as a body in pieces. The maker checked the tuning after we left and found it a little flat, which he said was the window.
