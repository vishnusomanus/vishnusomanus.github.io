Place

Across the still water

Jonah Ellison · 7 min

The boat leaves when the water is flat enough to see the far shore in it. There is a posted time, and then there is the time the hull actually moves. Regulars know which one to trust. Visitors arrive for the posted time and spend twenty minutes watching the mountains.

The man at the tiller has done the crossing since he was old enough to be trusted with other people’s coats. He does not narrate. If the wind comes down the valley he turns back, and the posted time is simply wrong for the rest of the morning. The refund is a nod and a later boat.

We rode three crossings and stayed for the one that did not happen. The interesting hour was the one spent at the jetty, with the bow still tied and the peaks going in and out of cloud. The photograph in the issue is from the crossing that did leave, looking forward because looking back made the passengers stiff.

On the far side there is a path and a hut that sells tea in paper cups. The boat waits forty minutes. That is the whole timetable. Everything else is weather.
