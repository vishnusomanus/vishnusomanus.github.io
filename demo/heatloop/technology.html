02Inside the store

Most of the energy hides in a melt, not a temperature.

Phase change

Our cells hold a salt hydrate that melts at 136°F. While it melts, it soaks up a large amount of energy without getting much hotter, so the store stays compact and loses less heat. Freezing gives that energy back at a steady temperature.

Vacuum-panel walls

Three-quarters of an inch of vacuum insulation panel lines every face of the store. In our lab tests a full Heatloop 28 loses less than 3% of its heat in a day, and that heat leaks into the room it stands in. Figures for other stores are typical values for comparison.

Controls that plan ahead

Each evening the controller reads tomorrow’s prices, the weather forecast and how your home used heat last week, then plans when to charge. Comfort always comes first: if the store runs low, it heats directly.

04Good questions

What engineers and neighbors ask us.

Is it the same as an electric thermal storage heater?+

No. A thermal storage heater warms one room and leaks its heat whether you want it or not. A Heatloop sits in a closet, basement or utility room, keeps its heat until it’s asked for, and feeds a whole hot-water heating system and your hot water.

Will it work with my radiators or baseboard?+

It delivers water at up to 144°F. Many radiator systems sized for a boiler handle that well, especially with modern controls, while baseboard often needs more length. Our home assessment checks what you have and tells you honestly if any of it would need upgrading.

What happens if the store runs out?+

The element heats water directly, like an electric boiler, so you are never left cold. You pay the going rate for that heat, which is why the controller plans to avoid it.

Is the material inside safe?+

The salt hydrate is non-flammable and sealed in welded cells inside a steel case. Nothing burns, and there is no combustion gas in your home. We are working through product certification with an independent testing lab and will publish the results.

Do I need a special rate plan?+

You’ll get the most from a plan with cheap hours, such as a time-of-use rate; ask your utility what it offers today. On a flat rate a Heatloop still works, but there is nothing cheaper to shift to. The estimator shows the difference.
