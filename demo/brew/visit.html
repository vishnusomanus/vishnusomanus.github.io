About & visit

A ten-barrel brewery in an old tire shop

Cinderjack started in 2017 with a secondhand brewhouse, two friends and a jackrabbit that lived under the loading dock. We’re still independent, still on the east side, and still brewing in small batches.

DirectionsMeet the crew

Our story

Small batches, on purpose

Dacey Prescott and Teodoro Arvizu met on a volunteer trail crew and spent the lunch breaks arguing about pilsner. In 2017 they signed a lease on a shuttered tire shop on NE Hawthorne, bought a ten-barrel brewhouse from a brewery in Idaho, and opened the roll-up door with four beers on tap.

The name came from the neighborhood: the cinder cones on the horizon, and the jackrabbit who watched the build-out from under the loading dock. We still brew everything on that same system, so batches are small and the board changes every couple of weeks.

One percent of every pint goes to trail and river groups across Central Oregon, and our spent grain feeds a cattle ranch out toward Alfalfa.

  1. 17

    The roll-up door opens

    Four beers, a borrowed food truck and 300 people on a Saturday.

  2. 19

    Cinder Trail gets a can

    Our first canning run: 40 cases, by hand, over two very long days.

  3. 21

    The beer garden

    We took over the lot next door: picnic tables, a fire pit and room for two food trucks.

  4. 23

    The barrel room

    Sixty oak barrels and two foeders for sours and the Lava Tube stout.

  5. Now

    Fifteen beers and counting

    Twelve taps, tours every weekend, and the jackrabbit’s great-grandkids.

The crew

Who’s brewing and pouring

  • Dacey Prescott

    Co-founder & head brewer

    Brewed in Portland and Fort Collins before talking Teo into an old tire shop. Writes every recipe on a legal pad first.

  • Teodoro Arvizu

    Co-founder & taproom

    Runs the front of house, the books and the trivia mic. Grew up in Prineville and knows every trail within an hour.

  • Ruthanne Ketchum

    Cellar & barrel program

    Looks after 60 barrels, the foeders and the sour blends. Can tell a happy barrel by knocking on it.

  • Kasimir Banfield

    Taproom manager

    Ten years behind bars from Astoria to Bend. In charge of the board, the playlist and the dog biscuits.

  • Paloma Mireles

    Brewer & tour lead

    Former river guide. Leads most of the weekend tours and brews Little Hare, our session hazy.

  • Cormac Bramwell

    Events & private parties

    Books the bands, the trucks and your rehearsal dinner. Ask him about the time a wedding party adopted a jackrabbit.

Getting here

715 NE Hawthorne Avenue

By car
Free parking behind the brewery, entrance on NE 10th Street. Overflow on NE Irving Avenue after 5pm.
By bike
Two bike racks and a repair stand out front. Ten minutes from downtown along the river trail and NE Hawthorne.
On foot
Fifteen minutes from downtown. The beer garden gate on NE 8th Street opens at the same time as the taproom.
Getting home
Ask the bar and we’ll call you a ride. You can leave your car overnight; just tell us at the bar.
