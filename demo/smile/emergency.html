Dental emergencies

In pain? We’ll see you today.

We keep emergency slots free every day we’re open, and the on-call dentist answers our after-hours line. Not sure how urgent it is? Use the symptom guide below.

Call (208) 555-0147 Book an emergency visit

After hours, call the same number and press 1 for the on-call dentist. Emergency exam & x-ray: $95.

Call 911 first if you have:

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing
  • Swelling spreading to your eye, neck or under your jaw
  • A head injury, or an injury from a serious accident
  • Heavy bleeding that won’t slow down, especially after an injury

Then call us once you’re safe, and we’ll arrange dental follow-up.

    While you wait

    Simple first steps for common problems

    General tips to keep you comfortable until you see a dentist. They don’t replace an examination.

    • Knocked-out adult tooth

      • Pick it up by the crown (the white part), not the root.
      • Rinse it briefly in milk or water. Don’t scrub it.
      • If you can, gently push it back into the socket and bite on a clean cloth. If not, keep it in milk.
      • Call us right away: the sooner, the better.

    • Chipped or broken tooth

      • Rinse your mouth with warm water.
      • Keep any pieces and bring them with you.
      • A cold compress on the cheek can help with swelling.
      • Avoid chewing on that side until you’re seen.

    • Toothache

      • Rinse with warm water and gently floss to remove trapped food.
      • Over-the-counter pain relief may help, if it’s safe for you. Follow the label.
      • Don’t put aspirin or pain-relief gel directly on the gum.
      • Book a visit if it lasts more than a day or two.

    • Lost filling or crown

      • Keep the crown somewhere safe and bring it in.
      • Avoid very hot, cold or sticky food on that side.
      • Don’t glue a crown back yourself.
      • Book a visit in the next few days, sooner if it hurts.

    • Swelling

      • Call us the same day for any swelling in the gum or face.
      • A cold compress on the outside of the cheek may ease it.
      • Call 911 if swelling spreads to your eye or neck, or you have trouble breathing or swallowing.

    • Braces, wire or aligner problem

      • Cover a poking wire with orthodontic wax or a small piece of sugar-free gum.
      • Lost or cracked aligner: wear your previous set and call us.
      • Never cut a wire yourself if it’s near your throat; call us.
