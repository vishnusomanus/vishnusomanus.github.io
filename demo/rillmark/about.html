About — the director and the bench Arch Street, Hartford

One director, a trusted bench

I’m Marika Drever. I grew up in Stonington drawing flip-books in the margins of tide tables, studied animation in Hartford, and spent eight years at broadcast and branding studios before starting Rillmark.

Rillmark is named after the marks the tide leaves in the sand: small, exact patterns made by something moving. That’s the job. I direct every project myself, from the first index card to the final master, and bring in specialists when an idea needs deeper craft than one pair of hands can give it.

I work with broadcasters, cultural organizations, nonprofits, independent bands and the odd fintech, mostly in New England, sometimes much further away.

How I work

Three rules I don’t break

  1. 01

    Every move has a verb

    If we can’t say what something does, surfaces, gathers, snaps, it doesn’t move yet.

  2. 02

    Paper before pixels

    Boards on index cards and an animatic come first, so changes are cheap while they still matter.

  3. 03

    Leave a usable thing

    Project files, templates and notes, organized so your team or the next studio can pick them up.

The bench

People I bring in

Freelancers I’ve worked with for years. They join when the idea needs them, and you see their names in the credits and in the estimate.

    The studio

    Second floor, Arch Street, looking at the river

    The studio is a converted shipping agent’s office, a 15-minute walk from the station. There’s a lightbox, a sound-treated corner for voice sessions, a 65-inch grading screen and a long table for clients who like to watch the boards go up.
