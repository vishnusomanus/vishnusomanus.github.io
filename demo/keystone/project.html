Malt House Lofts

Twenty years empty, one fire and a failing roof. We turned Walker’s Point’s last malt house into homes, without losing a single arched window.

The brief

The challenge

The malt house was built in 1898 to hold grain, not people. Its floors were rated for barley, not bathrooms; a 2011 fire had charred a third of the timber; and the city wanted the street front kept exactly as it was.

  • Brick walls up to 3 feet thick with no insulation
  • Timber floors too weak for residential loads
  • Historic district rules on every window and opening
  • River-edge site with a high water table

Our solution

We slid a new steel frame inside the old shell, so the brick only carries itself. Sound timber went back in as exposed beams; charred timber became the market hall’s counters.

  • Independent steel frame on 64 new helical piles
  • Inside-face wood-fiber insulation that lets the brick breathe
  • 212 arched windows rebuilt with slim double glazing
  • Ground-source heat pumps under the courtyard
