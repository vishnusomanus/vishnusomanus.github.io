Features

Everything between “job done” and money in the bank.

Tally covers the money side of a small creative business: quoting, invoicing, getting paid, logging what you spend and keeping books that make sense at tax time.

  • Invoicing
  • Expenses
  • Bookkeeping
  • Reports and taxes
  • Reminder writer

A tour of the four parts.

Quote it, invoice it, get paid.

Accepted quotes turn into invoices in one click. Clients pay by card, Apple Pay or ACH transfer from a link on the invoice, and reminders go out without you.

  • Quotes with e-signature and an expiry date
  • Recurring invoices for retainers, with deposits and part payments
  • US dollar, Canadian dollar and euro invoices, with the exchange rate recorded for you
  • Reminder schedules per client, written in your own words

Receipts that file themselves.

Photograph a receipt, forward an emailed bill to your Tally address, or let the bank feed spot it. Tally reads the vendor, date, total and sales tax for you to check.

  • Receipt capture in the mobile app and by email
  • Mileage log with the rates you set
  • Rebill expenses to a client with or without a markup
  • Team members submit, owners approve (Studio and Practice)

Bank feeds that match for you.

Payments in are matched to invoices; payments out are matched to receipts. You confirm, Tally files, and the books stay balanced without journal entries.

  • Read-only feeds from US business checking and savings accounts
  • Rules for regular payments: rent, software, insurance
  • Accountant access at no extra cost, with comments on any transaction
  • Full audit trail of every change

Reports you can actually read.

Profit and loss, aging, sales tax summaries on the cash or accrual basis, and exports your accountant can import. Tally prepares the figures; you or your accountant file them.

  • Sales tax summary per period, with the figures for each state
  • A rough tax set-aside so April isn’t a shock
  • CSV and PDF exports, plus a year-end pack for your accountant

Tally is bookkeeping software, not a tax advisor. Estimates are a guide only.

And the rest

The small things that add up.
