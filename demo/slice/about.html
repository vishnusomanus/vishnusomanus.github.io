Our story

A dough company that happens to make pizza

Crust Club started in 2016 with a borrowed horse trailer, a second-hand oven and a jar of sourdough starter. Ten years on, the starter is still going. So are we.

How it started

From horse trailer to Main Street

Nico Ferraro learned to stretch dough at his uncle’s pizzeria in Salerno. Bea Kowalski was a pastry chef who’d never owned a van. They met at a Sunday farmers market in Fair Haven, bought a horse trailer between them and spent a summer burning pizzas until they stopped burning them.

  1. 2016

    The horse trailer

    Weekend markets across Greater New Haven. One oven, two people, about 140 pizzas on a good Saturday.

  2. 2019

    Wooster Square opens

    A tiny shop in an old carriage-factory outbuilding, with a window for slices and a waiting list for the dough fridge.

  3. 2023

    Westville

    Our second shop, with a back patio, a bigger oven and our own riders on electric cargo bikes.

  4. Today

    38 people, one starter

    Around 1,900 pizzas a week, still from the same jar of Big Brenda we started with in 2016.

The dough

Four steps, two days, no shortcuts

  1. 01

    Feed Big Brenda

    Our starter gets flour and water at 7 AM every day. By lunchtime she’s bubbly and ready.

  2. 02

    Mix

    New York stoneground wheat, a little Italian 00, water, salt and Brenda. Mixed slowly, never warm.

  3. 03

    Wait 48 hours

    Balled by hand and left in the fridge for two days. Slow fermentation means flavor and an easy-to-digest crust.

  4. 04

    Bake for 90 seconds

    Stretched to order and baked at 850°F, so the crust puffs and blisters and the middle stays soft.

Sourcing

Who we buy from

Local where it makes sense, Italian where it really matters. We visit every supplier at least once a year.

Tomatoes

Campania, Italy

Plum tomatoes from a growers’ co-op near Salerno, crushed by hand in the kitchen every morning.

Flour

Upstate New York and Naples

Stoneground New York wheat from a family mill, blended with a little Italian 00 for stretch.

Basil & veg

Guilford, Connecticut

Herbs, zucchini and salad greens from a market garden 15 miles away. In winter, mostly from California.

Meat

Connecticut and Brooklyn

Free-range pork pepperoni and fennel sausage from a Connecticut butcher; nduja from a small producer in Brooklyn.

The people

Floury hands, big hearts

Things we won’t budge on

  • A living wage

    For every member of staff, riders included, plus all of the tips.

  • Compostable boxes

    Unbleached cardboard, vegetable inks, no plastic tables in the middle.

  • Leftover dough

    Goes to a food pantry in Fair Haven as focaccia every Monday.
