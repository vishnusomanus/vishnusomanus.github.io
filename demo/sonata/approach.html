Small beginnings, real connections

Built around a room,
not a syllabus.

Sonata began in 2017 with one upright piano, a borrowed coffee maker and a weekly guitar group. Neighbors brought songs they wanted to learn. The school grew from those conversations, and that is still how we begin.

Today our six teachers teach from a converted courtyard building on North Walnut Street. The rooms are small enough to feel familiar, with good instruments, sturdy music stands and somewhere comfortable to wait.

We teach by ear, by eye and by doing. Reading music can open doors, but it is not a test you have to pass before you are allowed to play. Your teacher will help you find the right balance.

Each term’s listening afternoons are invitations, never obligations. A duet with your teacher, one piece you have been working on, or just a seat in the audience: it all counts as being part of the music.

My practice days

Good habits, gentle expectations.

01 · Make it easy

Leave the music out

A safe instrument stand and an open book make starting easier. If unpacking takes five minutes, count that as part of practice.

02 · Make it specific

One tricky little bit

Slow down two measures. Try one chord change. Sing one phrase comfortably. Small tasks make progress easier to notice.

03 · Make it yours

Finish with something loved

Play a favorite song, improvise, or listen to a musician you admire. Enjoyment belongs in the plan.

Learning your way

Do you prepare students for exams and auditions?

Yes, where an exam or audition supports the student’s goals. Teachers can prepare students for performance and theory assessments and discuss which program fits. Entry fees and dates are agreed separately; there is no expectation to enter.

Can you adapt lessons for learning differences?

We start with a conversation about useful adjustments: visual plans, shorter instructions, a quieter room or breaks. We explain what we can offer before you decide. You can arrange a quiet visit outside teaching hours.

How do parents support practice?

For younger children, a predictable short routine helps more than correcting every note. Your teacher will show you one useful prompt. Please tell us if practice is becoming a source of stress.
