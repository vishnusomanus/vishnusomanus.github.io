A wind-down routine for people who hate routines

Forget the twelve-step evening. Three small things, done most nights, are plenty.

Read the guideClose the guide

Most “perfect evening routine” lists are written for people with no children, no commute and no deadlines. If that isn’t you, start smaller: pick one thing that signals the end of the day, one thing that relaxes your body and one thing that quiets your mind.

For many people that looks like dimming the lights at the same time each night, a few minutes of slow breathing, and reading something printed rather than lit. The order matters less than the repetition. After a couple of weeks, the first step starts to feel like a cue.

Missed a night? Nothing breaks. There are no streaks in Nightjar for a reason. Pick it up again tomorrow.

Bedroom5 min read · Oct 3, 2026

How to make your bedroom darker, cooler and quieter

Small, cheap changes to light, temperature and noise that many people find helpful.

Read the guideClose the guide

Light: blackout lining behind existing curtains is cheaper than new blinds, and a strip of foam weatherstripping along the top of a curtain rod stops the glow above. Cover standby lights with a small piece of tape.

Temperature: many people sleep better in a slightly cool room. Try a lighter comforter before turning the heat down, and open a window an inch if the street is quiet enough.

Noise: rugs, curtains and cushions absorb sound, and a steady background sound can mask sudden ones. That’s where a fan sound or brown noise can help.

Sounds4 min read · Sep 26, 2026

Brown, pink or white noise: which suits you?

They’re all steady “shh” sounds, but they feel very different. Here’s how to choose.

Read the guideClose the guide

White noise has equal energy across all frequencies, so it sounds bright, a bit like a hissing radio. It’s good at masking sharp sounds like voices.

Pink noise turns down the high frequencies, which makes it sound softer, more like steady rain. Brown noise goes further still and sounds deep and rumbly, like a distant waterfall.

There’s no right answer. Try each for a few nights at a low volume, just loud enough to blur the sounds you want to hide. You can preview all three on our sounds page.

The case for a gentler alarm

Why waking in a lighter moment can feel easier, and how to get the most from a wake window.

Read the guideClose the guide

Being woken suddenly from deeper sleep can leave you feeling groggy for a while. Many people find that waking at a lighter moment, near the end of a sleep cycle, feels smoother.

A smart alarm uses a window rather than a single time. Nightjar listens for movement in that window and starts a soft sound when you seem to be stirring, but never later than the time you set.

For best results, keep your phone on the nightstand, plugged in, rather than under your pillow.

Routines5 min read · Sep 12, 2026

Awake at 3am? What to try (and what not to)

Waking in the night is common. A few calm habits can make getting back to sleep easier.

Read the guideClose the guide

First, don’t check the time. Figuring out how many hours are left tends to wake the mind up. Nightjar’s red night mode keeps the screen dim if you do need it.

If you’ve been lying awake for what feels like a while, get up, go somewhere dim and do something calm and boring until you feel sleepy again, then go back to bed.

Occasional bad nights are normal. If you regularly wake and can’t get back to sleep, or it’s affecting your days, speak to a doctor. They can help in ways an app can’t.

Routines4 min read · Sep 5, 2026

Screens before bed: a realistic approach

You don’t have to ban your phone. You just have to stop it being the last thing you see.

Read the guideClose the guide

The trouble with phones at bedtime is less about the light and more about what’s on them: messages, news and endless feeds keep your mind busy.

Try a “last scroll” time 30 minutes before lights out. Charge your phone across the room, and let Nightjar’s sounds and alarm run from there.

If you read on a device, choose a dedicated e-reader or turn on a warm, dim screen setting.

Mornings5 min read · Aug 29, 2026

Getting up on dark winter mornings

Tips from our team in Sacramento, where December sunrise comes after 7am and tule fog can hide it for hours.

Read the guideClose the guide

When it’s still dark outside, your body has fewer clues that it’s morning. Turning on bright lights soon after waking, or sitting near a window once it’s light, can help.

Keep your wake time roughly the same every day, including weekends. A sunrise glow on your alarm screen and a gentle sound make the first minutes less jarring.

Low mood that comes and goes with the seasons is common. If winter regularly leaves you very low, talk to your doctor.
