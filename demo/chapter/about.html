About

A life on the marsh, and off it

Clemency Thorne was born above the bar of the Ship Tavern at Fennick, a place so remote that the beer came by tractor in winter. She learned to read from the backs of coasters and to listen from the regulars, who told the same stories every night and changed one small detail each time.

At eighteen she left for Salem to study history, and stayed. For eleven years she covered the courts for a newspaper in Newburyport, sitting through burglaries, frauds and, twice, murders. She says it taught her two things: that nobody tells the whole truth on a witness stand, and that the most important person in any courtroom is often the one in the back row who never says a word.

She wrote The Drowned Church on the commuter rail between Salem and Newburyport, a page a day in a reporter’s notebook. It won the Sandpiper Award for best debut, and Detective Sergeant Esme Lark has been asking her awkward questions ever since.

Clemency lives in a narrow house in Salem with her partner, a retired Coast Guard mechanic, and a rescue greyhound named Shingle who is afraid of the ocean. She swims at Salem Willows all year round, badly.

The places

Where the books are set

Every book is set within forty miles of where Clemency grew up. Most of the places are real; Fennick, its tavern and its landing are invented, though people on the Marsh have been known to give directions to them.

A writing life

  1. 2008

    Starts as a court reporter for a newspaper in Newburyport, covering superior and district courts across Essex County.

  2. 2016

    Her short story “The Sexton’s Daughter” wins a regional prize and finds her an agent, Sabine Corrigan.

  3. 2019

    The Drowned Church is published and wins the Sandpiper Award for best debut. Clemency leaves the courts to write full time.

  4. 2021

    The Cartographer’s Widow, her first historical novel, is a finalist for the Harrowell Prize.

  5. 2024

    Every Bell in Salem becomes her bestselling book, and is translated into fourteen languages.

  6. Now

    The Glass Marsh, the fourth Esme Lark mystery, and a fifth on the desk.

Questions people ask

Is Esme Lark based on a real detective?+

No, though she borrows the patience of several police officers Clemency watched give evidence, and the temper of none of them. Esme’s family tavern is a lot like the one Clemency grew up in.

Will there be more Esme Lark books?+

Yes. The fifth is being written now. Newsletter subscribers hear its title first.

Can I send you my manuscript?+

Clemency can’t read unpublished work, for legal reasons and because she would never get anything written. Agencies, including Winslow Greer, list how to submit on their own websites.

Is there going to be a TV series?+

The screen rights to the series have been optioned. That can mean a lot or nothing at all, so there is no news to share yet. Producers can write to the rights team.

How can I get a signed copy?+

Order from Turner Street Books in Salem and ask for a signed or inscribed copy. Clemency stops by every few weeks with a pen.
