Our story

An old dye works, full of new color

Mosaic is an independent nonprofit run by a small staff, a big team of volunteers and the people who come through the door. We believe a creative life shouldn’t depend on talent, money or knowing the right people.

How we got here

From empty yard to shared studio

It started with a question passed around a kitchen table: what if our city had somewhere to try things without having to be good at them first?

A handful of neighbors borrowed the keys to the old dye works, cleared the floor, found some chairs and started the coffee. A repair afternoon became a drawing group. The drawing group became an exhibition. People kept showing up.

Our program still grows from conversations rather than a grand plan. If you think something is missing, tell us at the welcome desk.

  1. 2013

    The dye works closes

    The last dyers leave the Race Street works after ninety years. The building sits empty for three winters.

  2. 2016

    A borrowed key

    A group of neighbors borrows the keys for a weekend repair café. Two hundred people show up.

  3. 2017

    Mosaic is born

    The group becomes a nonprofit, signs a lease and opens the Print Room with a second-hand press.

  4. 2020

    The Yard Room

    The old drying shed is rebuilt as a hall for gigs, film and dance, with a sprung floor.

  5. 2023

    Clay arrives

    A kiln, six wheels and a lot of shelving turn the back store into the Clay Studio.

  6. 2025

    Step-free throughout

    An elevator, new restrooms and a level yard make every room accessible.

What holds us together

Three things we keep coming back to

01

People before polish

You don’t need the right clothes, the right words or a portfolio. You should feel at home here exactly as you are.

02

Room to get it wrong

Trying matters more than a perfect result. We make space for experiments, happy accidents and starting again.

03

Everyone has something

A skill, a story, a spare hour or an excellent brownie recipe. Every kind of contribution counts.

How the doors stay open

Class fees, tickets and room rental pay for about two thirds of what we spend. The rest comes from 640 Friends, local foundations and the pay-what-you-can tin. Every booked class keeps two supported spots.
