06 / Our theater

Close to the story.
Close to each other.

Cuefold is a small, independent theater in Staunton’s old assembly hall. We believe a room full of people sharing a story is still a fairly remarkable thing.

A room with a new chapter

Once an assembly hall.
Always a meeting place.

Our building has held dances, meetings, arguments and celebrations. We’ve kept the proportions, opened up the first floor and given it a stage close enough to see a performer take a breath.

The program mixes work made here with visiting performances that deserve a closer look. Some nights are funny. Some are challenging. All of them begin with the same question: what might happen if we pay attention?

Look inside the program

The people who open the doors

A small team, a shared purpose.

Artistic director

Catriona Henshaw

Catriona shapes the program and directs work made in our rooms. She is interested in big questions told through small, surprising moments.

Audience & access

Melisande Alderton

Melisande looks after the welcome, from your first question to your trip home. She works with audiences to make practical improvements that last.

Production & participation

Anselm Trevaskis

Anselm makes things work on stage and brings people into the making. He runs our object-theater sessions and has a healthy respect for a well-labeled cable.

Room for the unfamiliar

We leave space in the program for first-time visitors and for work that doesn’t fit a neat description. Curiosity is a good enough reason to come.

Care is practical

Clear information, fairly priced tickets and a calm welcome matter. We listen to what makes a difference, and tell you honestly what we can offer.

Made with this place

Local people take part as audiences, volunteers, makers and partners. A small theater is strongest when the town can recognize itself in the room.
